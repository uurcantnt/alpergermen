A divorce case is filed in the Family Court at the domicile of either spouse. An uncontested divorce requires at least one year of marriage and a settlement agreement; in contested cases, fault, alimony, custody, property division and compensation claims are evaluated separately. The steps involved are set out on the divorce and family files page.

It is not mandatory in every case; however, it is required to appoint a defense attorney in crimes carrying a minimum sentence of more than five years imprisonment and in certain special circumstances. Establishing the defense from the investigation stage onward is critically important. How a defence is structured at each stage is explained on the representation in investigation and trial page.

In enforcement and attachment proceedings, the deadlines for objection, payment orders, precautionary attachment and litigation are extremely short and can result in loss of rights. A prompt legal assessment must be made upon receipt of service of process. How proceedings and objections run is covered on the enforcement file handling page.

A power of attorney is issued before a Turkish notary. For most files a general litigation power of attorney is enough: it covers filing suit, submitting pleadings, attending hearings and receiving service. Withdrawing a claim, admitting one, settling or referring a matter to arbitration each require an express special authority in the document. Divorce files require a power of attorney bearing a photograph, as noted on the authority in family files page. Clients abroad can have it issued at a Turkish consulate.

It depends on the type of dispute, the court's caseload, how quickly evidence is gathered and whether an expert report is needed. A straightforward debt claim and a contested divorce do not move on the same timetable. Appeal stages add further time after the first-instance judgment. No fixed duration is promised at the outset; a realistic range is given for each stage, and progress is reported as the file moves rather than only when it concludes.

For certain disputes mediation is a procedural precondition, which means a case cannot be filed until a mediator has been applied to. Employment claims and reinstatement requests, together with commercial debt claims, fall within this scope. Later amendments brought some tenancy disputes, dissolution of co-ownership and condominium matters into the same regime. Where no agreement is reached, the final minutes allow the matter to proceed to court. The employment side is set out on the mediation in employment claims page.

Two separate routes exist. Legal aid offices within the bar associations appoint a lawyer for those who cannot meet the fee; the application is made to the relevant bar and documents showing means are requested. Legal aid sought from the court itself is a different mechanism: it grants temporary exemption from court fees and litigation expenses and is decided within the case file. The two are assessed independently, each on its own conditions.

Istinaf is review by a regional court of justice. It examines both the law and the facts, may hold a hearing and can substitute its own decision for that of the first-instance court. Temyiz is review by the Court of Cassation and, as a rule, is confined to whether the law was correctly applied; the facts are not reassessed. Not every judgment can be taken to temyiz. The boundary is discussed in the limits of appeal after the regional court.

Court costs consist of application and judgment fees, service and postal charges, expert and site-inspection fees and witness expenses. An advance on costs is deposited with the court when the case is filed and expenses are met from it; if it runs out, a top-up is requested. As a rule the losing party bears these costs, and where a claim succeeds only in part they are apportioned. They are separate from the attorney's fee.

The process begins with a power of attorney, issued either before a Turkish notary or at a Turkish consulate abroad. Documents in another language must be filed with a sworn translation and, where required, an apostille. Meetings are held in Turkish or English. A judgment given by a foreign court produces no effect in Türkiye on its own; recognition or enforcement proceedings must be brought before it can be relied on here.

In most files followed through a lawyer the client need not attend; the hearing is handled by the attorney. There are, however, situations in which the statute requires the party to be heard in person — in an uncontested divorce the judge must hear both spouses directly. A court may also order a party's attendance where it considers this necessary. Any such requirement is identified at the outset and notified in advance.

The relationship rests on confidence, and a client may withdraw the authority at any time; a lawyer may likewise resign for good cause. Neither step suspends the stage the file has reached or the periods already running, so a handover should not be delayed. Once a new power of attorney is issued and the file documents are transferred, the matter continues from where it stood. Fees already earned by the previous lawyer are assessed separately.

Hearing dates, interim decisions and submissions filed are reported to the client as they arise. Parties resident in Türkiye can also view their own files through the UYAP citizen portal via e-Devlet, while clients abroad are updated directly. What a given procedural term means, and what is expected at the next stage, is explained during meetings. Anything significant is passed on without waiting for a scheduled update.

The document can be issued at the Turkish consulate in your country of residence. Meetings are held by telephone or video link and documents can be sent digitally, so litigation, enforcement proceedings and administrative applications can all be followed without travelling to Türkiye.

The date of service usually starts the clock, so keep the envelope and note the date it was delivered. What the document is — a statement of claim, a payment order, notice of an administrative act or a judgment — determines which route applies. Periods for replying, objecting or appealing are short, and once missed the outcome can become final. In enforcement matters the period for objecting to a payment order is particularly tight.
