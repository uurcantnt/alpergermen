How the right area is identified

Which branch of law a dispute belongs to is rarely obvious at first sight. The same set of facts can be advanced as a contractual claim or as a claim in tort, and the choice changes the burden of proof, the court with jurisdiction and the periods that apply. For a client unfamiliar with Turkish procedure this distinction is easy to miss, and it is not one that can be corrected late in the day.

The real work in a first meeting is therefore characterisation: placing the file within the correct branch. The chronology of events, the nature of the relationship between the parties and the documents in hand are weighed together. Where a contract exists its terms govern; where none does, the conduct of the parties is examined instead.

Getting this wrong has consequences. A case may be dismissed for want of jurisdiction, or a claim refused because the period for bringing it has passed. Characterisation is accordingly not a preference but a finding drawn from the file, and the reasoning behind it is explained before anything is filed.

Files that cut across several areas

In practice most files do not sit within a single area. A divorce brings with it the liquidation of the matrimonial property regime, a claim that engages family law and property law at once and is usually heard as a separate case.

A workplace accident can generate three parallel processes: employment claims together with compensation for pecuniary and non-pecuniary loss, proceedings involving the Social Security Institution, and a criminal investigation into the employer. These are heard by different courts yet feed into one another; the employment limb is set out on the workplace accidents and employment claims page.

Property left by a deceased person follows a similar path. Succession and division files begin in inheritance law, continue as proceedings for the dissolution of co-ownership and end in a sale through the enforcement office. Disputes between company shareholders can likewise reach into contract and enforcement law alongside commercial law.

In files of this kind the order in which claims are brought affects the outcome directly. One case may need to be stayed pending another, or two processes may have to run side by side. The roadmap is therefore built from the file as a whole rather than from a single claim.
