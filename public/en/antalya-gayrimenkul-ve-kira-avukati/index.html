— Real Estate and Tenancy Law

Real Estate and Tenancy Lawyer in Antalya

In property disputes the land registry record, the contract and the payment history are decisive. Litigation in tenancy and ownership disputes, together with pre-contract review for property transactions, is provided in Antalya.

Scope of services

Eviction of leased property, determination and adaptation of rent, collection of rent arrears, title deed cancellation and registration, dissolution of co-ownership, pre-emption and prevention of interference actions are handled.

Tenancy relationships are assessed under the Turkish Code of Obligations (Law No. 6098) and ownership disputes under the Turkish Civil Code. Disputes arising from condominium ownership fall under Law No. 634 on Condominium Ownership.

Before a property purchase, the land registry record, annotations and declarations, zoning status and any mortgage or attachment entries are reviewed as a pre-contract risk assessment.

Grounds for eviction

Ending a lease depends on specific grounds. Those arising from the landlord include a genuine need for the dwelling or business premises and reconstruction or substantial renovation. For a new owner, eviction on grounds of need is subject to periods running from the date of acquisition.

Grounds arising from the tenant include two justified warnings for non-payment of rent, an undertaking to vacate and use of the property contrary to the contract.

Where rent is unpaid, enforcement proceedings with a request for eviction may be initiated through the enforcement office. If no objection is filed in time or the debt remains unpaid, eviction may be requested.

Rent determination and increases

In an action to redetermine rent, the location, size and age of the property and comparable rents in the vicinity are taken into account. The court generally orders an expert examination and applies an assessment of equity.

Where determination is sought for a new period, sending a warning before the start of that period, or filing within it, matters if the decision is to apply from the beginning of the new period.

In long-standing tenancies, where circumstances have changed significantly, a request for adaptation may arise. Such a request must be supported by concrete and measurable data.

How the process runs

The title record, contract and payment documents are reviewed to determine the legal nature of the dispute.

Where necessary a notice is served through a notary; its content and the date of service are recorded.

Depending on the type of dispute, an action is filed before the civil court of peace or the civil court of first instance; some tenancy disputes require mediation before filing.

Requests for site inspection, expert examination and witness evidence are submitted.

Appellate remedies are assessed and enforcement of the final decision is followed up.

Documents required

The title deed or a current land registry extract showing the legal status of the property.

The lease agreement, its annexes and any undertaking to vacate.

Bank receipts and payment records evidencing rent payments.

Notarial warnings and proof of service, showing that time-bound rights were exercised.

Zoning status, building permit and occupancy certificate, together with any condominium management plan.

Time limits

In residential and roofed business premises leases, termination by the tenant requires notice a certain period before expiry. Without notice the contract renews automatically.

For the landlord to seek eviction on grounds of need, the periods laid down in the law must be observed; separate periods apply to an owner who acquires the property later.

The right of pre-emption is subject to a forfeiture period running from notification of the sale; once it expires the right can no longer be exercised.

In title cancellation and registration actions, how time runs depends on the legal ground relied upon, so each file requires separate assessment.

Common mistakes

Paying rent without a description or in cash, which makes it difficult to prove which month is covered.

Signing the undertaking to vacate on the same date as the lease, which casts doubt on its validity.

Drafting the warning notice incompletely, which may prevent the condition of two justified warnings from arising.

Not reviewing annotations, mortgages and attachments before transfer, which can lead to consequences that are difficult to reverse.

Filing a rent determination claim without researching comparable rents, which may lead to partial dismissal.
