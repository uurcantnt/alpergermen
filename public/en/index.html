04 Legal Support

Legal support in Antalya:
how the office works.

Every file begins by identifying what the dispute actually requires. At the first meeting the background is discussed, the documents are reviewed and the possible legal routes are compared. Litigation is not always the first option; mediation or a contractual solution may be more appropriate.

Once the route is set, steps subject to time limits are prioritised. Many rights must be exercised within short periods that begin once documents are served, so the calendar is drawn up at the outset.

Meetings are held in person at the office in Toros Neighbourhood, Konyaaltı, and by telephone or online for clients outside the city or abroad. Meetings can be conducted in Turkish and English.

05 How a File Runs

From the first meeting
to the decision.

First meeting. Appointments are made by telephone or email. Bringing documents such as a contract, title record, payroll or a served envelope allows a more accurate assessment. The legal nature of the dispute and the available routes are explained at this stage.

Power of attorney. A power of attorney is issued before a notary. Depending on the file a general power of attorney may suffice, while divorce cases require one bearing a photograph. Clients abroad may have it issued through a Turkish consulate.

Proceedings. Petitions are prepared, evidence is submitted and hearings are followed. Where a file also requires enforcement proceedings, an administrative application or mediation, these run in parallel.

Updates. Hearing and application dates are shared in advance and developments are reported in plain terms. After the judgment, appellate remedies are assessed.

06 Fees

How legal fees
are determined.

Legal fees are determined by assessing the scope of the work, the time and effort involved and the nature of the file. The Minimum Attorney Fee Tariff published by the Union of Turkish Bar Associations is taken as the basis; it sets the lowest amount that may be charged for each type of work.

Fees are explained before the meeting and set out in a written attorney agreement covering the scope of the work, payment terms and which party bears the litigation costs. Court fees, service costs and court-appointed expert witness fees are separate from the attorney fee.

The attorney fee awarded by the court is a different item from the fee agreed between the parties. Understanding this distinction keeps the financial side of the process predictable.
