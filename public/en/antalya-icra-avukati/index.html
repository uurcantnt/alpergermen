— Enforcement and Bankruptcy Law

Enforcement Lawyer in Antalya

Time limits in enforcement law are short, and most rights must be exercised within a few days of service. Proceedings and litigation are conducted in Antalya for both creditors and debtors under the Enforcement and Bankruptcy Law No. 2004.

Scope of services

Enforcement proceedings with and without a judgment, proceedings specific to negotiable instruments, proceedings for the eviction of leased property and enforcement of pledges are initiated and pursued.

On the debtor side, objections to a payment order, objections to the debt and to the signature, negative declaratory and recovery actions and complaints regarding unattachable assets are prepared.

Obtaining precautionary attachment orders, applying attachments, following valuation and sale stages and challenging the ranking schedule are also covered.

Types of proceedings

Proceedings without a judgment are used by a creditor who holds no court decision. If the debtor objects to the payment order in time, the proceedings stop automatically, and the creditor must then seek annulment or removal of the objection.

Proceedings with a judgment rest on a court decision or a document equivalent to a judgment. Here an objection does not stop the proceedings; only a decision suspending enforcement can do so.

In proceedings based on cheques, bonds and bills of exchange the periods are shorter and an objection does not, as a rule, stop the proceedings. Acting without delay after service is therefore important.

Objections and available actions

An objection to a payment order is filed with the enforcement office within the short statutory period from service. Its grounds and scope directly affect the next stage, and an objection to the signature must be raised separately and expressly.

The creditor may continue by bringing an action for annulment of the objection or, where a qualifying document exists, by seeking removal of the objection. Compensation against the unsuccessful party may arise in these actions.

The debtor may bring a negative declaratory action to establish that no debt is owed. Where the debt has already been paid, the action takes the form of a recovery action. Suspension of the proceedings against security may be requested.

Attachment and sale

Once the proceedings become final, attachment may be applied to the debtor's movable and immovable property, bank accounts, salary and rights held by third parties. Assets that cannot be attached are listed separately in the law.

Attached assets are valued. The valuation may be challenged by way of complaint within the prescribed period; a figure that does not reflect reality directly affects the sale price.

Sale is carried out by electronic public auction. The proceeds are distributed taking account of pledges and priority claims, and where there are several creditors a ranking schedule is drawn up, which may be challenged.

Documents required

The contract, invoice, cheque, bond or court judgment on which the claim is based.

The debtor's identity and address details and, where known, tax number.

Bank receipts, vouchers and release documents where payment is alleged.

On the debtor side, the envelope and date of service, needed to calculate the objection period.

In proceedings on negotiable instruments, the original instrument and any protest documents.

Time limits

In proceedings without a judgment, the period for objecting to a payment order is seven days from service. After that no objection can be made and the proceedings become final.

In proceedings specific to negotiable instruments the objection period is five days and an objection does not, as a rule, stop the proceedings.

An action for annulment of an objection must be brought within one year of service of the objection. If that period passes, the creditor must sue under the general provisions.

Sale of attached assets must be requested within the prescribed periods; if it is not, the attachment lapses.

Common mistakes

Not opening the served documents or not notifying a change of address, so that the objection period passes unnoticed.

Failing to state expressly in the objection that the signature is disputed, so that the signature is deemed accepted.

Making payments without a description, which makes it difficult to prove which debt was settled.

Starting proceedings before an enforcement office lacking jurisdiction, leading to an objection and lost time.

Not requesting the sale in time, causing the attachment to lapse.
