Divorce Lawyer in Antalya

A divorce file rarely concerns only the end of a marriage; custody, alimony, division of matrimonial property and compensation claims are usually assessed together. These proceedings are conducted in Antalya as a member of the Antalya Bar Association.

Scope of services

Uncontested and contested divorce cases, custody and personal relationship arrangements, child and spousal maintenance claims, pecuniary and non-pecuniary compensation requests and the liquidation of the matrimonial property regime are handled.

Proceedings are conducted under the Turkish Civil Code. Where violence or a risk of violence exists, protective and preventive measures under Law No. 6284 on the Protection of the Family and Prevention of Violence Against Women may also be requested.

Recognition and enforcement actions are also covered, so that a divorce decree issued by a foreign court can take effect in Türkiye.

Uncontested and contested divorce

In an uncontested divorce the spouses agree on every heading — custody, maintenance, compensation and property division — and record that agreement in a protocol. The marriage must have lasted at least one year, the spouses must apply jointly or one must accept the other's claim, and the judge must hear the parties in person and be satisfied that their declarations are free.

In a contested divorce the allegations must be supported by evidence. Witness statements, message and call records, bank movements, social media content and, where applicable, forensic reports shape the file. Evidence must have been obtained lawfully.

Maintenance and compensation claims

Interim maintenance may be requested for the duration of proceedings, both for the child and for a spouse who would otherwise face financial hardship. After the decision becomes final, child maintenance and, where the conditions are met, spousal maintenance come into play.

The amount is determined by considering the parties' income, the age and needs of the child and the principle of equity. If circumstances later change, a separate action may be brought to increase or reduce it.

A spouse whose personal rights have been harmed by the events leading to the divorce may claim non-pecuniary compensation; a spouse who is not at fault or less at fault and whose existing or expected interests are impaired may claim pecuniary compensation. These claims must be raised expressly in the petition.

How the process runs

In the first meeting the background, the requests and the available documents are reviewed, and the appropriate type of action is identified.

The petition is prepared with the claims and the underlying facts set out concretely, and filed with the competent family court.

Interim custody, interim maintenance and, where relevant, protection measures are requested during the proceedings.

The list of evidence is submitted, witnesses are heard and, where needed, an expert examination or a social investigation report is requested.

After the judgment, appellate remedies are assessed and registration of the final decision with the civil registry is followed up.

Documents required

Identity documents and, where available, a copy of the marriage certificate, together with the identity details of the spouses and children.

Documents showing income — payroll records, social security statements, tax records — which are used when calculating maintenance and compensation.

Where liquidation of the property regime is requested: title deeds, vehicle registrations, bank statements and any loan agreements.

Messages, photographs, forensic reports and witness details supporting the allegations. Bringing these to the first meeting allows the process to be planned sooner.

Time limits

How long a divorce takes depends on whether the case is contested, the number of witnesses, whether an expert examination is required and the court's caseload. For this reason no commitment as to duration can be given.

Certain periods are nevertheless fixed by law. The period for lodging an appeal runs from service of the judgment; missing it causes the decision to become final.

Forfeiture periods apply to allegations of forgiveness and to specific grounds such as adultery. An assessment shortly after the events become known is therefore advisable.

Common mistakes

Filing before evidence has been gathered, which may leave allegations unproven and lead to a different assessment of fault than expected.

An incomplete uncontested divorce protocol, which may result in separate actions later for maintenance or property division.

Not raising the liquidation of the matrimonial property regime in good time, which can prolong the overall process.

Submitting recordings obtained unlawfully, which may lead to the evidence being disregarded and to separate liability.

Not following up on service of documents — the most frequent cause of missed deadlines.

Frequently Asked Questions

Divorce cases are heard by the family court. Jurisdiction lies with the court of the domicile of either spouse or of the place where they last resided together for six months. For parties residing in Antalya, the Antalya Family Courts are competent.

The marriage must have lasted at least one year, the spouses must apply jointly or one must accept the other's claim, and the judge must hear the parties in person and be satisfied that their declarations are free. A protocol covering custody, maintenance, compensation and property division must also be approved by the judge.

The duration depends on whether the case is contested, the number of witnesses to be heard, whether an expert examination is required and the court's caseload. No period can be stated without reviewing the specifics of the file.

The governing criterion is the best interests of the child. The child's age, educational and health needs, the parents' capacity to provide care and their relationship with the child are assessed. The court may order an expert examination and hears the views of a child capable of forming them.

Protective and preventive measures may be requested under Law No. 6284. This application can be made independently of the divorce case, and measures may also be requested through law enforcement or the administrative authority.

Liquidation of the matrimonial property regime is the subject of a separate action and, as a rule, the divorce decision must first become final. Raising the claim in good time and planning the evidence together nevertheless keeps the process coherent.
