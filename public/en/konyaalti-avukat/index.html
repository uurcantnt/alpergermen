Disputes frequently seen in Konyaaltı

Because residential and tourism investment is concentrated in the district, disputes arising from tenancy relationships and problems relating to property transactions arise often. Reviewing the position before a transfer helps prevent later disputes.

The weight of the tourism and service sectors also makes employment disputes common. Seasonal work, overtime and determination of service are recurring headings in these files.

Property acquisition by foreign nationals, residence permit and citizenship applications and the recognition of foreign court decisions are also frequently encountered.

Areas of practice

In family law: divorce, custody, maintenance and liquidation of the matrimonial property regime. In criminal law: defence during investigation and prosecution, and representation of victims.

In employment law: severance and notice pay, reinstatement and employment receivables. In real estate law: eviction, rent determination and title cancellation and registration.

In enforcement law: proceedings and objections. In inheritance law: division and abatement actions. In commercial law: corporate matters and collection of commercial receivables.

Proceedings in Antalya

Depending on the nature of the dispute, cases filed in Antalya are heard by the family, civil, commercial, labour, enforcement or criminal courts. For residents of Konyaaltı, the Antalya courts have jurisdiction in most files.

Appeals against first instance decisions are examined by the Antalya Regional Court of Appeal. As appellate periods are strict, the date of service of the decision is recorded in the file.

In files that meet the conditions, attendance at hearings may also be possible by audio and video transmission. Whether this is available is assessed on a file-by-file basis.

How a meeting works

Appointments are made by telephone or email, and documents relating to the file are requested beforehand.

At the first meeting the background and the requests are discussed and the available documents reviewed; the appropriate legal route is identified together.

The documents needed are listed, and any step subject to a time limit is planned as a priority.

Fees are determined on the basis of the Minimum Attorney Fee Tariff according to the scope of the work and set out in a written agreement.

Developments in the file are reported regularly, and hearing and application dates are shared in advance.

What to bring to the first meeting

Identity documents and, where applicable, details of a power of attorney.

Written documents relating to the dispute: contract, title record, invoice, payroll or bank records.

Any petition, payment order, warning notice or decision that has been served, together with the envelope.

Printed or digital copies of correspondence, messages and photographs.

If a file has previously been opened, its number and the court concerned.

Common mistakes

Waiting after documents have been served — the most frequent cause of losing time-bound rights.

Attending a meeting without gathering the documents, which leads to an incomplete assessment.

Not obtaining a legal review before signing a contract, which can lead to consequences that are difficult to reverse.

Seeking advice only after the dispute has escalated, which reduces the prospect of settlement and prolongs the process.
