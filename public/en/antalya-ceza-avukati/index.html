Criminal Defence Lawyer in Antalya

In criminal proceedings the decisive stage is often the investigation, before any statement is taken. Reviewing the file and planning the defence at that point affects everything that follows. Defence and victim representation are conducted in Antalya as a member of the Antalya Bar Association.

Scope of services

Representation as defence counsel before law enforcement and the public prosecutor during the investigation, defence before criminal courts of first instance and assize courts during prosecution, and representation of victims and intervening parties.

Proceedings are conducted under the Turkish Penal Code (Law No. 5237) and the Code of Criminal Procedure (Law No. 5271). Files arising from traffic accidents, cybercrime, offences against property and offences against persons are handled within this scope.

Objections against protective measures such as detention, judicial control and seizure are prepared, and requests concerning the execution stage are assessed.

Defence during the investigation

The statement and interrogation set the direction of the file. A suspect has the right to remain silent, the right to defence counsel and the right to be informed of the accusation. Exercising these rights can prevent consequences that are difficult to reverse.

Whether a restriction order applies to the investigation file is checked; where none applies, a copy is obtained and the basis of the accusation is assessed. The prosecutor may be asked to collect exculpatory evidence.

Whether the custody period stayed within statutory limits and whether arrest and search procedures complied with the rules are examined. Evidence obtained unlawfully cannot form the basis of a judgment.

Mediation and alternative routes

For offences listed in the law, a mediation procedure applies during the investigation and prosecution stages. Mediation rests on agreement between the parties before a mediator; where agreement is reached, a decision of non-prosecution is issued or the case is dismissed.

Whether to accept a mediation offer should not be decided before its consequences are understood. If the offer is declined, the investigation continues in the ordinary course.

In files that meet the conditions, deferral of the announcement of the verdict, conversion into an alternative sanction or suspension may arise. Each of these institutions has different conditions and different consequences and is assessed file by file.

How the process runs

The file is reviewed to identify the alleged act, the supporting evidence and any protective measures.

Counsel attends the statement or interrogation; the scope of the defence is planned in advance.

Where detention or judicial control has been ordered, an objection is prepared within the statutory period.

After the indictment is accepted, the trial defence is prepared and requests for witnesses, expert examination and site inspection are filed.

Appeal and, where available, cassation applications are made; for final decisions, requests concerning execution are followed up.

Documents required

Any statement records, arrest and custody reports and documents that have been served.

The investigation or file number, needed to locate the file within the relevant unit.

Photographs, camera footage, message and call records relating to the incident, together with witness details.

In traffic accident files: the accident report, alcohol test results and any forensic report; where injury is involved, hospital records.

Time limits

Time limits in criminal proceedings are strict and missing them causes loss of rights. An objection to a detention order must be lodged within the short period prescribed by law from the moment the decision becomes known.

Objections to a decision of non-prosecution and appeals against a judgment are likewise subject to time limits. The date of service should therefore always be recorded.

For offences subject to complaint, a complaint period runs from the moment the act and the offender become known. Once it expires no investigation can be opened.

The overall duration depends on the number of defendants and witnesses, expert examinations and the court's caseload, and cannot be stated in advance.

Common mistakes

Giving a statement without defence counsel present, which may narrow the defence at later stages.

Making explanations before the legal characterisation of the accusation is understood, which can steer the file in another direction.

Failing to track objection and application periods, which leads to applications being rejected without examination of the merits.

Not requesting the collection of exculpatory evidence, which may leave the file incompletely examined.

Not updating the address for service, which allows decisions to become final without being learned of.
